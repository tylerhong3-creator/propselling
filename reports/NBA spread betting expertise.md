# Closing lines set the bar for NBA bettors

A credible NBA spread expert starts from one fact: **across four decades of scholarship, the NBA closing point spread is an unbiased, weak-form efficient forecast of the final margin once the vig is counted.** No peer-reviewed study shows a model or rule that consistently beats closing NBA spreads out of sample. The documented anomalies are real but small and niche: big home underdogs, momentum overreaction, sentiment shading toward popular teams, early-season totals and the 2020 COVID regime shift. Most fade once published and few clear the **52.38% break-even** that standard −110 pricing imposes. The expert's working toolkit therefore sits around that efficient price. It includes exact vig and no-vig arithmetic, closing line value (CLV) as the main measure of skill, an empirical NBA margin distribution (residual standard deviation of about 12.6 points, weak key numbers, half-points worth about 1.5–2.5 percentage points), fractional Kelly staking, and a realistic view of sample size. A true 55% bettor needs roughly 1,400 bets before results alone become statistically convincing. Situational factors such as rest, travel, altitude, injuries and tanking move margins, but closing lines largely price them. Any remaining edge comes from timing (acting before news is priced) and from shopping for better prices, not from structural "systems." The market is also shaped by bookmakers who take positions instead of balancing their books, by US retail sportsbooks that limit winning accounts, and by an integrity threat that now centers on player props and inside injury information rather than on full-game spreads.

## Read the evidence with three caveats

The research behind this report had three limits, and a reader should weigh the findings accordingly. First, network restrictions blocked most full-text papers. Many figures therefore come from abstracts, bibliographic pages (RePEc/IDEAS) or secondary summaries, and exact effect sizes for several classic papers (Camerer 1989, Entine & Small 2008, Stern 1994's fitted parameters) could not be retrieved. Second, many NBA-specific numbers in the betting-math sections are **a researcher's own calculations** from a public closing-odds archive covering 2011-12 to 2021-22 (SportsbookReviewsOnline data re-hosted on GitHub) ([Dataset](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flancast90/sportsbookreview-scraper/main/data/nba_archive_10Y.json)). After dropping corrupt rows, 12,956 games remained. The archive has closing spreads and totals but **no spread prices**, so every against-the-spread (ATS) figure assumes −110. Its moneylines carry a 3.2–4.5% overround, so they reflect a retail or consensus market rather than Pinnacle, and nothing after 2021-22 was checked. Third, much practitioner lore, including back-to-back point values, star-player values and teaser folklore, comes from betting media with no published data. The report labels those claims. The scholarly frame on efficiency is well established. The precise current-era coefficients are less so.

## Four decades of research find spreads efficient but not flawless

### Statistical efficiency holds while betting rules sometimes win

The founding literature drew a distinction that still organizes the field. Gandar et al. (1988), the NFL template later applied to the NBA, found that statistical tests could not reject market rationality, but some economic (betting-rule) tests did reject it ([scite](https://scite.ai/authors/john-m-gandar-RVlwWp)). Sauer's 1998 *Journal of Economic Literature* survey concluded that wagering prices are **"to a first approximation, efficient forecasts of outcomes,"** with "important (and interesting) exceptions" ([EconBiz](https://econbiz.de/Record/the-economics-of-wagering-markets-sauer-raymond/10005560587)). Researchers use three standard tests. The first is an OLS regression of margin on spread, testing the joint hypothesis α=0, β=1. The second is a probit or logit model of covering. The third is a betting simulation judged against 52.38%, often with likelihood-ratio tests of a "fair bet" (50%) null against a "no profitability" (52.38%) null. Because of that gap, a market can fail the fair-bet test and still be economically efficient. One five-season NBA study confirmed weak-form efficiency and rejected semi-strong efficiency, yet its simulated strategies still won below 52.4% ([Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/27860819/Is_the_NBA_betting_market_efficient)).

The researcher's archive shows the same efficiency in recent data. At closing lines from 2011-22, **home teams covered 49.3% and favorites 50.0%**, and the mean residual (margin minus closing spread) was −0.20 points ([Dataset](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flancast90/sportsbookreview-scraper/main/data/nba_archive_10Y.json)).

### Five biases recur, and each is small

The best-replicated NBA anomaly concerns **large favorites and big home underdogs**. Paul & Weinbach (2005) studied 1995-96 to 2001-02 and found that bettors overbet large favorites. Betting big home underdogs rejected both the fair-bet and the no-profit nulls ([RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jospec:v:6:y:2005:i:4:p:390-400)). An out-of-sample retest on 2000-08 data found "strong support for an efficient market." The one surviving exception was **home underdogs of 10 or more points** ([The Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/nba-gambling-inefficiencies-a-second-look/)). The exact cover rates were not visible in the material the researchers could reach.

The second bias is **hot-hand and momentum overreaction**. Camerer's 1989 *AER* paper asked whether bettors believe in the hot hand and found a small bias ([Caltech](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20110211-141748791)). Brown & Sauer (1993) countered that streaks partly reflect real changes in team strength, so a line that moves with a streak is not by itself evidence of bias ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/r/aea/aecrev/v83y1993i5p1377-86.html)). Later work settled the question in a consistent way. Waggoner et al. (2014) used 34 seasons and found that streaks move both the spread and the actual margin ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jijfss/v2y2014i4p359-370d42745.html)). Arkes (2011) found that real momentum exists but that gamblers "significantly overstate" it ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bpj/jqsprt/v7y2011i3n13.html)). For the 1983-86 data, the error was "too small to be profitably exploited" ([The Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/the-effect-of-momentum-on-the-nba-point-spread-market/)). Moskowitz's 2021 *Journal of Finance* study covers 18,681 NBA games within about 120,000 contracts across four leagues. It finds momentum consistent with delayed overreaction and a weaker value effect, but returns that **"fail to overcome transaction costs"** ([Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/what-sports-betting-teaches-us-about-financial-markets); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:76:y:2021:i:6:p:3153-3209)).

The third bias is **sentiment shading**. Feddersen, Humphreys & Soebbing (2016) used about three decades of NBA data and found that bookmakers raise prices on popular home teams. Avoiding popular sides still did not produce long-run profits ([University of Alberta](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/folio/2019/08/how-bookmakers-tip-sports-betting-odds-in-their-favour.html)).

The fourth bias concerns **totals and season timing**. Bettors prefer overs, and books shade totals upward ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/buc/jpredm/v4y2010i2p15-22.html)). NBA totals are significantly biased early in the season, and a simple strategy won **56.72%** against closing totals. **Spread lines showed no comparable early-season bias** ([ETSU](https://dc.etsu.edu/etsu-works/17780)). In a later sample (2009-2012), 58.2% of week-one games finished under the total, though the totals market was efficient over the full season ([UWF](https://ircommons.uwf.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Early-season-NBA-overunder-bias/99380090347306600)). Preseason spreads in 2005-2014 were too large, which made underdogs profitable in exhibition games but not in the regular season ([Applied Economics 2016](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/00036846.2016.1213367)).

The fifth bias is **regime-change mispricing**. A preprint finds that NBA moneylines became "very inefficient" during COVID. Betting every underdog from the bubble to the end of the 2020 season returned **16.7%**, while other leagues stayed efficient ([arXiv 2109.07581](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.07581)). A European basketball study found that bookmakers there correctly anticipated the drop in home advantage, so the effect was not universal ([Unibo](https://cris.unibo.it/bitstream/11585/894931/4/s10479-022-04950-7.pdf)).

### Anomalies decay roughly as fast as they are published

Each edge has shrunk over time. Camerer's streak effect was untradeable even in its own data. The broad Paul & Weinbach underdog effect narrowed to +10 home dogs within a decade. The playoff "zig-zag" (betting the team that lost the previous playoff game) went 53.0% ATS from 1991 to 2015 but **51.6% since 2001**, which is below break-even ([Boyd's Bets](https://www.boydsbets.com/nba-playoffs-zig-zag-theory/)). These are betting-media records, not peer-reviewed results. The pattern also appears in newer markets. A 2026 study of Polymarket NBA contracts found single-market anomalies "exceedingly rare" ([arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00864v1)). A neural network trained on about 16,000 NBA games lost roughly 1.5% against Pinnacle closing moneylines ([arXiv 2107.08827](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08827)). The steelman for the opposing view is a Miami University thesis. Using ten online books, it reports profitable strategies that are amplified where markups are lower ([OhioLINK](https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file/send?accession=miami1533158563565605&disposition=inline)). The size of the vig decides whether a statistically real bias can be traded profitably. The literature has one major gap: **no peer-reviewed study tests NBA ATS anomalies on post-2018 US legal-market data**.

### Bookmakers take positions instead of balancing

Levitt (2004) overturned the textbook picture of a bookmaker who balances action and collects the vig. Using NFL contest wagers, he showed that bookmakers forecast better than bettors and **set prices away from market-clearing levels to exploit predictable biases**, taking sizable positions ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ecj/econjl/v114y2004i495p223-246.html)). Paul & Weinbach (2008) extended this to the NBA with real betting percentages from four sportsbooks for 2004-05 to 2006-07. Favorites, especially road favorites, drew a disproportionate share of bets, and books priced those biases into the spread. Unlike the NFL, though, **simply fading the public did not beat efficiency in the NBA** ([FIT Publishing](https://fitpublishing.com/node/944); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/jsf/intjsf/v3y2008i3p137-145.html)). The practical lesson is that books shade just enough to profit from the biased side without leaving the contrarian side above 52.4%. "Fade the public" is therefore roughly fair, but not profitable after the vig.

## The arithmetic of a −110 market leaves little margin for error

### The vig and break-even rates

Every expert calculation starts from the break-even rate. Converting American odds A to decimal odds D gives D = 1 + 100/|A| for favorites and D = 1 + A/100 for underdogs, and the break-even probability is 1/D. A two-way market's overround is the booksum B = Σ1/Dᵢ. The theoretical hold, meaning the book's margin when action is balanced in proportion to fair probabilities, is 1 − 1/B. The table below shows computed values.

| Price (both sides) | Break-even | Overround | Hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| −110 | **52.38%** | 4.76% | **4.55%** |
| −108 | 51.92% | 3.85% | 3.70% |
| −105 (reduced juice) | 51.22% | 2.44% | 2.38% |
| −102 | 50.50% | 0.99% | 0.98% |

At −110 a coin-flip bettor loses 4.55 cents per dollar, exactly the hold. Moving to −105 lowers the break-even by **1.16 percentage points**. That is worth more than most handicapping edges, which is why line and price shopping matter so much. In the researcher's archive, closing NBA moneylines had a median overround of 3.2–4.5%, highest near pick'em games ([Dataset](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flancast90/sportsbookreview-scraper/main/data/nba_archive_10Y.json)). The researchers could not verify the commonly quoted Pinnacle NBA spread margin of about 2–2.5%.

### Removing the vig

To get fair (no-vig) probabilities, the main methods are multiplicative normalization (pᵢ = rᵢ/B), additive, power, odds-ratio and Shin. Shin's model assumes some bettors are insiders, and **for a two-outcome market Shin and additive give identical answers** ([implied package vignette](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opisthokonta/implied/master/vignettes/introduction.Rmd)). Štrumbelj (2014, *International Journal of Forecasting*) found Shin-derived probabilities more accurate than basic normalization ([Springer, citing Štrumbelj](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-022-04722-3)). The full-text accuracy metrics were not checked. For a −110/−110 spread every method returns 50/50, so the choice matters only for moneylines and lopsided prices. For a −150/+130 moneyline the methods differ by about 0.3 percentage points: multiplicative gives 58.0% to the favorite and Shin/additive give 58.3% (computed).

### Closing line value as the evidence of skill

CLV compares the number a bettor took with the closing number. In points it is simply (your line − closing line) from your side. In price terms it is (your decimal odds ÷ the closing no-vig fair odds) − 1, which equals the bet's expected ROI if the close is the true probability. Practitioners, including Joseph Buchdahl in secondary summaries, treat Pinnacle's no-vig close as the best available estimate of true probability ([secondary summary of Buchdahl](https://pod-d614f9k2t-pod-team.vercel.app/blog/closing-line-value--clv-demystified-by-expert-joseph-buchdahl); [Action Network](https://actionnetwork.com/how-to-bet-on-sports/general/closing-line-value-definition-importance-sports-betting)). **No peer-reviewed study validating CLV as a predictor of profit was found.** Skeptics include Karl Whelan, and Buchdahl himself reportedly noted that Pinnacle's close lost reliability in major European soccer leagues ([Karl Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)).

The archive offers the clearest NBA-specific evidence. The researcher took the side the line later moved toward and graded it at the opening number, assuming −110 ([Dataset](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flancast90/sportsbookreview-scraper/main/data/nba_archive_10Y.json)):

| Open→close move | Bets | ATS win % at opening number |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 0.5 pt | 10,550 | **54.0%** |
| ≥ 1.0 pt | 6,767 | 55.6% |
| ≥ 2.0 pt | 2,219 | 60.7% |
| ≥ 3.0 pt | 715 | 64.6% |
| Same sides at the closing number | 10,567 | **49.6%** |

This is a look-ahead exercise, since nobody knows the move in advance, and opening limits are low. It is not a strategy. It does show the mechanism: **the closing line absorbs information the opener lacked**, so beating the close by one to two points corresponded to a 55–61% win rate. This fits Moskowitz's finding that line moves tend to persist rather than reverse ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/value-momentum-sports-betting/)). Under the normal model, each half-point of spread CLV near the center is worth about **1.6 percentage points** of cover probability (computed).

### The margin distribution and weak key numbers

The archive data give the expert's core distributional facts ([Dataset](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flancast90/sportsbookreview-scraper/main/data/nba_archive_10Y.json)). The final margin minus the closing spread had **SD 12.61** and was close to normal: 70.0% of games fell within one SD, against 68.3% for a normal distribution. The distribution was **more peaked and fatter-tailed, with excess kurtosis of +2.63**. The SD rose over time, from about 12.0 in 2011-15 to **13.8 in 2020-21 and 13.6 in 2021-22**. Whether it rose further in the 2022-26 high-scoring era is unverified. This computed figure also resolves a muddle in practitioner sources. Forum posts cite a closing-line mean absolute error of about 9.3 points ([SBR forum](https://www.sportsbookreview.com/forum/handicapper-think-tank/59267-1-point-lines-in-american-sports)), which some posters misremember as the SD. For a roughly normal distribution, an MAE of 9.3 implies an SD of about 11.6, the same order as the measured value and the maximum-likelihood probit σ of 11.6.

**Key numbers are weak in the NBA.** The most common absolute margins were 7 (6.49% of games), then 5, 6 and 8 at about 6% each, and 3 at 5.74%. Across all integer closing spreads, the push rate was just **3.35%**, peaking near 4.1–4.5% at 3, 6 and 7. The per-number samples are small, with standard errors of about 0.7–1.3 points. In the NFL, by contrast, 3 is the final margin in about **15%** of games and pushes at −3 occur about 9% of the time ([DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/nfl-margin-of-victory-data-1999-2025)). An NBA half-point is worth about half its landing probability: roughly 2.3 percentage points near 7 and 1.2–2.0 near 1–4. An NFL half-point through 3 is worth about 4.5 points.

### Converting a spread to a win probability

The classic conversion treats the margin as normal around the spread, so P(favorite wins) = Φ(spread/σ). This follows Stern's Brownian-motion model, fitted to 493 NBA games from 1991-92 ([JASA](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476851)), and Polson & Stern's method of backing out implied volatility from spread and moneyline together ([De Gruyter](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jqas-2014-0095/html)). Market moneylines in the archive show that **no single σ fits**. The implied σ falls from about 15–18 for 2–3 point favorites to about 10.5 for 12–14 point favorites ([Dataset](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flancast90/sportsbookreview-scraper/main/data/nba_archive_10Y.json)). This follows from the peaked, fat-tailed residuals: big favorites win outright more often than a σ=12.6 normal predicts.

| Home closing spread | No-vig market win prob | Normal model, σ=12.6 | Implied σ |
|---|---|---|---|
| −3 | .571 | .594 | 14.9 |
| −5 | .643 | .654 | 12.9 |
| −7 | .715 | .711 | 11.8 |
| −10 | .816 | .786 | 10.9 |
| −13 | .887 | .844 | 10.5 |

An expert should therefore use an empirical or market-calibrated mapping, not one σ. The archive also supports a scaling rule: the margin SD grows with the total, from about 12.1 at totals near 190–210 to **14.1 at 230+**, or roughly 0.059 × total ([Dataset](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flancast90/sportsbookreview-scraper/main/data/nba_archive_10Y.json)). This is consistent with variance rising with the number of possessions. In a high-total game a σ-based conversion should widen, which helps the underdog's chance of winning outright.

### Spread–total correlation, teasers and buying points

Full-game NBA sides and totals are essentially independent. Across 12,440 non-push games, the correlation between "favorite covers" and "over" was **−0.014**, and for favorites of 10 or more points it was −0.033 (not significant) ([Dataset](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flancast90/sportsbookreview-scraper/main/data/nba_archive_10Y.json)). A favorite-plus-over same-game parlay has no structural correlation edge at the full-game level. Correlations in first-half lines and player props were not tested.

Teasers fail in the NBA for the same reason key numbers are weak. **Six-point teaser legs won 70.35%** in 2011-22, against a per-leg break-even of 72.37% for a two-team teaser at −110. No segment of favorites or underdogs cleared break-even by a significant margin ([Dataset](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flancast90/sportsbookreview-scraper/main/data/nba_archive_10Y.json)). The NFL "Wong teaser" logic depends on crossing 3 and 7, which together account for about 24% of NFL margins ([DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/nfl-margin-of-victory-data-1999-2025)), and it does not carry over. Buying a half-point for 10 cents (moving from −110 to −120) pays only if the landing probability exceeds **3.6%**. In the NBA that threshold is met roughly only at 6 and 7. Buying at 5 cents (−115) is worthwhile at almost any number, and buying at 15 cents is never worthwhile (computed). These conclusions use closing lines and assumed prices, not actual book teaser menus.

## Kelly staking and sample size demand patience and humility

The Kelly criterion maximizes long-run log growth: for net odds b, f* = (bp − q)/b, where p is the win probability and q = 1 − p. At −110 (b = 0.909) that means a full-Kelly stake of **1.3% of bankroll at a true 53%, 3.4% at 54% and 5.5% at 55%** (computed). MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba document both the "good" property, asymptotically maximal growth, and the "bad" one: full Kelly "can be very volatile," with a probability of only about **0.67 of doubling before halving** ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/h/wsi/wschap/9789814293501_0039.html)). A continuous approximation reproduces that 0.67 and shows the trade-offs of fractional Kelly (computed):

| Kelly fraction | Growth vs. full | P(double before halve) | P(ever down 50%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 | 44% | 99.2% | 0.8% |
| 0.50 | 75% | 88.9% | 12.5% |
| 1.00 | 100% | 66.7% | 50.0% |
| 2.00 | 0% | 50.0% | 100% |

Estimation error is the decisive argument for betting a fraction of Kelly. A bettor who believes p = 0.55 when the truth is 0.5375 is staking **twice the true Kelly fraction**, and at twice Kelly expected growth is zero. Half Kelly keeps three-quarters of the growth while cutting the risk of halving the bankroll from 50% to 12.5%. That asymmetry is why professionals bet a quarter to a half of Kelly. Simultaneous bets on one slate, and correlated positions such as the same side at several books, should be sized jointly, not each at full individual Kelly.

Sample size is where most self-assessments fail. At −110 the standard error of a win rate is about 50%/√N: ±3.2 points at 250 bets and ±1.6 at 1,000. The number of bets at which a bettor's *expected* z-score against 52.38% reaches 1.96 is about **1,400 for a true 55% bettor, 3,700 for 54% and 25,000 for 53%** (computed). Those figures give only about 50% statistical power, and 80% power needs roughly twice as many bets. Luck alone puts a no-skill 50% bettor at 55% or better over 250 bets **5.7%** of the time. A full NBA season has about 1,230 games, so even a strong bettor who bet every game would sit only about two standard errors above break-even after a year. This is the practical case for CLV. Its per-bet variance is far smaller than win/loss variance, and a secondary summary attributes to Buchdahl the claim that a consistent ~5% CLV edge can show significance in about 50 bets, compared with thousands needed from results alone ([secondary summary](https://pod-d614f9k2t-pod-team.vercel.app/blog/closing-line-value--clv-demystified-by-expert-joseph-buchdahl)). That figure is unverified at the primary source. It also implies a warning about touts: among many handicappers, some will show 55%+ records over hundreds of picks purely by chance.

## Situational factors move margins, but closing lines price most of them

### Home court has shrunk to about two points

NBA home advantage has declined substantially. *Scorecasting* reported home teams winning 62.7% of games, mostly in 1990s-2000s data ([Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/2015/june/home-field-advantage-the-facts-and-the-fiction)). Recent seasons run about 54–57%, but that figure comes only from non-peer-reviewed blogs and betting sites ([Sparkle Technologies](https://sparkletechnologies.com/blog/nba-disappearing-home-court-advantage)). The empty-arena seasons provided a natural experiment. In 2020-21, home teams won **50.60% without crowds and 58.65% with them**, and with fans they outscored opponents by **2.18 points** ([Monash](https://www.monash.edu/turner-institute/news-and-events/latest-news/2023-articles/its-official-home-crowd-advantage-is-a-thing)). The archive's 2011-22 average home margin was +2.68 against a mean closing home spread of −2.88 ([Dataset](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flancast90/sportsbookreview-scraper/main/data/nba_archive_10Y.json)). FiveThirtyEight originally set home court at 100 Elo points, about 3.5 points, and later cut it as the effect shrank ([FiveThirtyEight](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/how-we-calculate-nba-elo-ratings); [nicidob analysis](https://nicidob.github.io/nba_elo/)). A defensible current generic value is **about 2–2.5 points**, and 3+ points is outdated except for specific venues. Referee home bias in subjective calls was historically the leading explanation ([Chicago Booth Review](https://chicagobooth.edu/review/home-field-advantage-facts-and-fiction)), but it has been "almost eliminated" since 2020 ([arXiv 2210.13687](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.13687)). The remaining gap probably owes more to schedule and travel effects, which the league has also been reducing. No source cleanly separates these causes for 2023-26.

### Rest, travel and altitude are real but modest

Entine & Small (2008) found that road teams' rest deficits are "an important contributor" to home advantage, though "not a dominant factor" ([RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bpj:jqsprt:v:4:y:2008:i:2:n:6)). Their point coefficients could not be retrieved. The only peer-reviewed evidence of market mispricing is older. Ashman, Bowman & Lambrinos (2010) found home teams underperforming the spread on the second night of back-to-backs, with the effect magnified after eastward travel ([RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jospec:v:11:y:2010:i:6:p:602-613)). FiveThirtyEight's model charged 46 Elo points for playing on consecutive days, which the researcher converts to roughly 1.5–2 points ([nicidob](https://nicidob.github.io/nba_elo/)). The circadian evidence conflicts. Roy & Forest (2018) found **westward** travel hurt visiting teams in evening games ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jsr.12565)), while a 2022 *Frontiers* study found **eastward** jet lag hurt home teams returning from western trips ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2022.892681/full)). Altitude has firmer support. Harris & Roebber (2019) found Denver and Utah have the largest home advantages ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0220630)), and FiveThirtyEight gave Denver an altitude bonus of about 47 Elo points ([FiveThirtyEight](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/how-our-2015-16-nba-predictions-work)). Reasonable modeling priors, not established facts, are a back-to-back penalty of about 1–2 points and a Denver premium of about 1–1.5 points above generic home court. Utah's premium is uncertain. **No credible post-2015 study shows a persistent, exploitable rest or travel edge at the close**, and betting media agree these edges have compressed. Once the schedule is public, the market prices it. What remains is timing: news about rest decisions that arrives after the opener.

### Injuries are the largest repricing event

Oddsmakers during the 2025 playoffs valued LeBron James at about **4 points** and players such as Austin Reaves and Jalen Williams at about 1 point or less ([theScore](https://www.thescore.com/news/3531319/nba-playoff-injury-woes-how-much-are-stars-worth-to-their-teams)). Betting-media tiers of 4–7 points for MVP-level players and 2.5–4 for All-Stars are unsourced heuristics. A star's market value sits below his raw on/off differential because replacement minutes absorb part of the loss. The 2023 Player Participation Policy cut star absences by about 15% in its first year ([Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/basketball/2024/nba-awards-load-management-adam-silver-1234775529/)). In 2024-25, however, games missed to injury rose 13%, and the share of back-to-backs where a star played only one game rose from 22% to 24% ([Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/basketball/2025/nba-injuries-load-management-data-participation-1234849899/)). The 65-game awards threshold creates predictable incentives: stars near the threshold tend to play, while stars already ineligible or on teams with nothing at stake tend to sit. Soccer exchange evidence shows that public news is priced "swiftly and fully" ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:econjl:v:124:y:2014:i:575:p:62-91)). No academic study measures NBA line response to injury reports, so the size of any edge from fast reaction to news is unmeasured.

### Referees, tanking and playoffs

Price & Wolfers documented opposite-race foul bias in 1991-2002 ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w13206)). The bias disappeared after media exposure ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/awareness-reduces-racial-bias/)), and a replication found it non-significant for 2007-2010 ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5137224_Racial_Discrimination_among_NBA_Referees)). Crew foul-rate tendencies, which mainly affect totals, matter more today than bias, but no credible source quantifies a referee-crew ATS effect. On tanking, Soebbing & Humphreys found that "betting markets believe that tanking takes place in the NBA" ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1465-7287.2011.00298.x)). A thesis summary reports line adjustments of 3+ points for eliminated teams ([Alberta thesis](https://central.bac-lac.gc.ca/.item?app=Library&id=TC-AEU-2112&oclc_number=750203113&op=pdf)). Data-mined tanking "systems" in betting media, such as a reported 58.5% ATS fade of bad late-season home dogs ([Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nba/a-profitable-nba-tanking-strategy)), carry heavy multiple-testing risk. A single-season student test found no post-All-Star mispricing ([GitHub](https://github.com/asj005/QSS20-Project)). Teams that have locked in a playoff seed show some letdown inefficiency ([UGA](https://openjournals.libs.uga.edu/fsr/article/download/3242/2876/9324)). The playoff zig-zag has decayed to below break-even, and **no credible evidence supports revenge or look-ahead spots**.

## Models approach the market but rarely beat it

Modern team ratings are variations on one equation: rating difference plus home court equals expected margin. FiveThirtyEight's NBA Elo used **K = 20**, a margin-of-victory multiplier ((MOV+3)^0.8 / (7.5 + 0.006·Elo difference), confirmed verbatim only on its WNBA page), and carried **75% of each rating** into the next season ([FiveThirtyEight](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/how-we-calculate-nba-elo-ratings); [WNBA methodology](https://fivethirtyeight.com/methodology/how-our-wnba-predictions-work/)). Its 100 Elo ≈ 3.5 points implies roughly 28 Elo points per point of spread, a derived conversion. SRS (schedule-adjusted margin of victory) and least-squares or Massey ratings are linear relatives of Elo ([Sports-Reference](https://www.sports-reference.com/blog/2015/03/srs-calculation-details/); [Massey](https://masseyratings.com/theory/massey97.pdf)). Dean Oliver's Four Factors weight shooting about 40%, turnovers 25%, offensive rebounding 20% and free throws 15% ([Basketball-Reference](https://basketball-reference.com/about/factors.html)). Player-based metrics (RAPM, EPM, DARKO, LEBRON) are the main route to injury adjustment through player value × projected minutes, but **no published head-to-head test of their game-level predictive accuracy was found**.

The peer-reviewed forecasting record points the same way. Harville's 1980 least-squares model, on NFL games, reached a mean absolute error of 10.68 against the bookmaker's 10.49 ([JASA](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/01621459.1980.10477504)). Štrumbelj & Vračar's possession-based Markov model performed comparably to other models, but "bookmaker odds were the best probabilistic forecasts" ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:intfor:v:28:y:2012:i:2:p:532-542)). Manner's (2016) state-space model with home court and back-to-back effects found the spread market "hard to beat." Combining the model with odds yielded only "slight improvements," and team strength varied persistently only across seasons, not within them ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bpj:jqsprt:v:12:y:2016:i:1:p:31-41:n:4)). That last result is a warning against heavy recency weighting. The most cited profitable result, Hubáček, Šourek & Železný (2019), reported consistently positive NBA profits for 2007-2014. The model was not more accurate than the market. It worked by **decorrelating its predictions from bookmaker probabilities** and sizing bets with portfolio theory ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:intfor:v:35:y:2019:i:2:p:783-796)). Its odds source and closing-line benchmark could not be confirmed. Walsh & Joshi (2024) found that selecting models by calibration rather than accuracy improved betting returns ([Bath](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/machine-learning-for-sports-betting-should-model-selection-be-bas/)). Their reported +34.69% versus −35.17% ROI is implausibly large for a real market and should be treated as unverified.

The practical modeling discipline follows from this record. Start each season from a prior that reverts partly to the mean, using preseason win totals or player projections. Shrink noisy components: opponent 3P% over a team's first 25 games correlated only **0.24** with its rate over the next 57, against 0.66 for opponent 2P% ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/nba/2021/2/12/22279459/nba-make-miss-3-point-shooting)). Validate walk-forward against **closing** lines, using only information available before tip-off. That includes injury news as known at the time, not box-score minutes, and player metrics as of that date, since many public plus-minus values are fit retrospectively on future data. Judge the model by log loss, Brier score and CLV, not by straight-up accuracy. Free data include Princeton's Historical Sports Odds Database, which has NBA opening and closing spreads and totals for 2006-2023 ([Princeton DSS](https://dss.princeton.edu/catalog/resource6741)). Cross-checking two odds sources catches sign and pick'em errors.

## Sharp books shape prices while retail books limit winners

Practitioner sources describe a two-tier market. Low-margin, high-limit market makers, with Pinnacle the canonical example, open at low limits, let sharp action shape the number, and raise limits as the price converges ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Pinnacle_Sports); [Complete Sports](https://www.completesports.com/how-pinnacle-sets-the-sharpest-lines/)). A third-party claim that Pinnacle opens at about 25% of maximum limits is unconfirmed. Retail US books largely follow those prices and manage risk by profiling customers. Academic soccer evidence supports the convergence idea: prices close to kickoff predict better than prices 24 hours earlier ([Angelini, De Angelis & Singleton](https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/8c5e3d3d-d36f-4279-b566-da91767d1a5c/information_efficiency_angelini_de_angelis_singleton.pdf)). **NBA-specific line-release timing has no credible source.** Reverse line movement and "steam" are practitioner concepts with no peer-reviewed NBA test. Public betting-percentage feeds are unaudited, unlike the actual sportsbook data used in academic studies.

Massachusetts provides the best regulatory data on limiting. As of December 10, 2024, **0.64% of sportsbook accounts were limited**. Of those, **12.7% were capped below 1% of the default maximum stake and 57.6% at 1–24%**, and winning bettors were more likely to be limited ([MGC materials](https://massgaming.com/wp-content/uploads/Meeting-Materials-9.30.25-OPEN.pdf); [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/massachusetts-limiting-sports-betting-prediction-markets-exchanges-sharps-box-september-2025)). The commission's data request asked operators about closing-line value, which suggests CLV is both the bettor's skill metric and the book's flag for sharp accounts. A rule requiring books to notify limited bettors was reportedly approved in December 2025, but its June 2026 effective date is unconfirmed ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/massachusetts-bettors-could-soon-have-wagerning-limitations-explained-dec-18-2025)).

The post-PASPA US market reached a record **$16.96B in revenue on $166.94B of handle in 2025** ([AGA](https://www.americangaming.org/commercial-gaming-revenue-hits-78-7-billion-in-2025-driving-record-18-1-billion/)). Legal Sports Report calculates an implied national hold of about **10.2%**, up from 6.9% in 2019. Commercial handle fell about 2% in Q1 2026, the first quarterly decline on record, as prediction markets drew volume ([Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/263300/sportsbooks-make-more-money-from-slower-sports-betting-growth-in-2025/)). The rise in hold reflects a shift toward parlays. The relevant margin for a straight NBA spread is still about 4.5% at −110. No regulator source gave the NBA's share of handle.

## Integrity threats target props and inside information, not spreads

The scholarly debate over forensic detection of point shaving should make an expert cautious about reading fixing into spread distributions. Wolfers (2006) found that heavy NCAA favorites more often won without covering than expected, and inferred that about 1% of games might be shaved ([Berkeley copy](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/PointShaving.pdf)). Borghesi then found the same pattern in the NBA and NFL. Bernhardt & Heston found it even in games with no betting line, and late-game strategy in blowouts explains it ([J. Sports Analytics review](https://doi.org/10.3233%2FJSA-150011); [Illinois](https://news.illinois.edu/researchers-dispute-study-pointing-to-college-basketball-point-shaving/)). Asymmetric outcomes around the spread are therefore weak evidence of corruption.

The proven NBA cases involved insiders and narrow markets. Referee Tim Donaghy was sentenced in 2008 to 15 months; one NPR report says 13, which conflicts with the other sources. The NBA-commissioned Pedowitz Report concluded he acted alone ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/donaghys-surprising-sentence-15-months); [Washington Times](https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2008/oct/2/report-donaghy-acted-alone/)). **Jontay Porter** was banned for life on April 17, 2024, and pleaded guilty to rigging his own exits to hit "under" props ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/jontay-porter-pleads-guilty-in-case-tied-to-gambling-scandal-that-tanked-his-nba-career)). On **October 23, 2025**, federal prosecutors in Brooklyn charged 34 people in two indictments. One alleges Terry Rozier signaled an early exit in a March 2023 game so associates could bet his unders, and that Damon Jones sold non-public Lakers injury information. The other, a poker case, names Chauncey Billups ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/miami-heats-rozier-trail-blazers-billups-arrested-in-federal-gambling-probe-sources-say); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/illegal-gambling-nba-poker-betting-chauncey-billups-terry-rozier-rcna239497)). Jones pleaded guilty in April 2026 ([NBC Sports](https://www.nbcsports.com/nba/news/damon-jones-pleads-guilty-in-federal-gambling-probe-terry-rozier-to-face-additional-charges)). A superseding indictment alleges a **$100,000 bribe** to Rozier, whose trial is set for February 8, 2027 ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/terry-rozier-pleads-not-guilty-to-bribery-charges-awaits-february-trial)). These case facts come from major news outlets; the Justice Department's own releases could not be fetched. The league and its partner sportsbooks responded by restricting player-prop markets. They first removed unders for two-way players ([ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/40140497/jontay-porter-scandal-nba-leagues-betting-risks)). By 2025-26 they had dropped props on two-way and 10-day players and restricted turnover, foul and missed-free-throw props ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/sports/sports-gambling/nba-mlb-microbets-sports-betting-gambling-rcna224412?rand=14095)). In October 2026 they reportedly agreed to limit multi-stat under parlays, "first of" bets and unders on players averaging under 12 minutes ([Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/sports/2026/10/16/nba-sportsbooks-agree-to-ban-several-types-of-player-prop-bets-amid-gambling-concerns/)). That last report rests on a search summary, and the participating books were not verified. A single player controls his own minutes far more easily than a final margin, so **the manipulation risk sits in low-salary players' props and in early access to injury information**. For a spread bettor, this matters in one specific way: non-public availability news is exactly the edge the market cannot price, and trading on it can be criminal.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "expertise" means for this market. It is not mainly the ability to pick winners. It is the ability to price accurately against an efficient benchmark and to judge one's own results honestly. The expert's real edge is narrow. It comes from paying less vig, which is worth more than most handicapping ideas. It comes from betting before information reaches the close, with CLV as the evidence that this is happening. And it comes from information the market under-weights, which is why Hubáček's decorrelation result matters more than any accuracy contest. The NBA's statistical profile also undermines several transplanted NFL habits. With weak key numbers, near-zero correlation between sides and totals, and a fat-tailed but evenly spread margin distribution, Wong teasers, routine point-buying and correlated favorite-plus-over parlays lose much of their logic, while accurate spread-to-probability mapping becomes more important.

Two questions should shape future work. First, nobody has published a rigorous test of NBA spread efficiency on post-2018 US legal data, in a market where retail books limit winners, parlays dominate hold and the margin SD appears to be rising with three-point volume. That gap is the main opportunity for a bettor with data. The current-era SD, home court value and rest coefficients should be re-estimated from 2022-26 closing lines, not taken from older literature. Second, as integrity enforcement moves toward account-level monitoring and CLV-based profiling, the measure that proves a bettor's skill is also the one that gets the account limited. A credible long-term operation must plan for limited access to retail books, through low-margin market makers, exchanges or prediction markets, as carefully as it plans its models.
