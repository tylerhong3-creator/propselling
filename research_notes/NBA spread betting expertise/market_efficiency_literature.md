Full-text fetches were blocked by the session's network allowlist (mdpi.com, thesportjournal.org, rdes.it and econpapers.repec.org all failed to resolve). ## Q1. What did the foundational studies find?

### Takeaway
The founding work (Camerer 1989; Brown & Sauer 1993; Gandar et al. 1988 for the NFL; Sauer's 1998 JEL survey) found that sports betting prices are efficient "to a first approximation." Statistical (OLS) tests usually cannot reject efficiency, but economic and betting tests, plus streak tests, show small biases. These biases are usually too small to beat the −110 vig.

### Cited Findings
- **Camerer, C. (1989), "Does the Basketball Market Believe in the 'Hot Hand'?", *American Economic Review* 79(5): 1257–61.** The paper asks whether NBA point-spread bettors believe in the hot hand (Gilovich, Vallone & Tversky 1985). It proposes betting against teams on winning streaks. Later work describes the result as a *small* hot-hand bias in basketball betting markets — [Caltech record](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20110211-141748791); [summary of later citing literature](https://ideas.repec.org/r/aea/aecrev/v79y1989i5p1257-61.html)
- **Brown, W.O. & Sauer, R.D. (1993), "Does the Basketball Market Believe in the Hot Hand? Comment," *AER* 83(5): 1377–86.** This comment could not reject Camerer's hypothesis. It argued that streak-related changes in team performance appear to be reflected in the final spread, so part of the "hot hand" pricing may be rational — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/r/aea/aecrev/v83y1993i5p1377-86.html); [search summary of citing work](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20110211-141748791)
- **Brown, W.O. & Sauer, R.D. (1993), "Fundamentals or Noise? Evidence from the Professional Basketball Betting Market," *Journal of Finance* 48(4): 1193–1209.** This paper uses the NBA betting market to study unexplained asset-price volatility (how much of the spread's movement is fundamentals and how much is noise) — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v48y1993i4p1193-1209.html)
- **Gandar, J., Zuber, R., O'Brien, T. & Russo, B. (1988), "Testing Rationality in the Point Spread Betting Market," *Journal of Finance* 43(4): 995–1008 (some reference lists give 995–1007), DOI 10.2307/2328148.** This is an NFL study, not an NBA one, but it is the template for NBA tests. Its statistical tests cannot reject market rationality, while its economic (betting-rule) tests do reject it — [Scilit](https://scilit.com/publications/c990659542ad56b524e376acaa847c94); [scite abstract](https://scite.ai/authors/john-m-gandar-RVlwWp)
- **Sauer, R.D. (1998), "The Economics of Wagering Markets," *Journal of Economic Literature* 36(4): 2021–2064.** Wagering-market prices are "to a first approximation, efficient forecasts of outcomes," with "important (and interesting) exceptions." Models with diverse information, heterogeneous agents and transaction costs help explain the exceptions. Section 5 covers the North American football point-spread market. A corrigendum appeared in *JEL* 37(2) (1999). Sauer also wrote a 2005 follow-up in the *Journal of Economics and Finance* — [EconBiz](https://econbiz.de/Record/the-economics-of-wagering-markets-sauer-raymond/10005560587); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:aea:jeclit:v:36:y:1998:i:4:p:2021-2064); [corrigendum](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/jeclit/v37y1999i2p567-567.html)
- **Gray, P.K. & Gray, S.F. (1997), "Testing Market Efficiency: Evidence from the NFL Sports Betting Market," *Journal of Finance* 52(4): 1725–37.** This NFL comparison replaces OLS with a probit model. Probit-based strategies earn statistically significant profits *in sample*, but documented inefficiencies "tend to dissipate over time." A secondary source says Vergin & Sosik confirm that the regular-season home-underdog bias was eliminated — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v52y1997i4p1725-37.html); [EconBiz](https://econbiz.de/Record/testing-market-efficiency-evidence-from-the-nfl-sports-betting-market-gray-philip/10005214612)
- **Paul, R.J. & Weinbach, A.P. (2005), "Bettor Misperceptions in the NBA: The Overbetting of Large Favorites and the 'Hot Hand'," *Journal of Sports Economics* 6(4): 390–400.** Covering the 1995–96 to 2001–02 seasons, the paper finds systematic bettor misperceptions in the NBA point-spread market: large favorites are overbet. Betting *big home underdogs* rejects both a fair bet and the null of no profitability. Betting against winning streaks was reported as profitable — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jospec:v:6:y:2005:i:4:p:390-400); [search summary of the study](https://www.academia.edu/27860819/Is_the_NBA_betting_market_efficient)

### Inferences
- The recurring pattern is that *statistical* efficiency (spread is an unbiased predictor of margin) usually holds, while *economic* efficiency (no rule beats 52.38%) is sometimes rejected in specific subsets: big favorites/home dogs, streaks, early-season totals.
- Brown & Sauer show that a price moving with streaks is not, on its own, evidence of bias. The question is whether outcomes move as much as the price does.

### Gaps
- Exact ATS percentages and sample sizes for Camerer (1989) and Brown & Sauer (1993) could not be retrieved (full text not reachable). UNVERIFIED recollection: Camerer used roughly three NBA seasons (about 1983–86) and found teams on long winning streaks covered slightly less than 50%, but the difference was not exploitable after vig.
- **Vergin, Tassoni, Osborne, Kochman & Goodwin:** no source confirming the NBA-specific content of these papers turned up. Vergin appears only as a citation (Vergin & Scriabin 1978 on favorites; Vergin & Sosik on the NFL home underdog). Treat any claims about these authors as unconfirmed. UNVERIFIED recollections: Tassoni (1996, *Journal of Gambling Studies*) on a "point-spread underdog" or home-dog anomaly in NBA/NFL; Osborne (2001, *JSE*) on NBA overreaction; Kochman & Goodwin (2004, *Journal of Gambling Studies*) on NBA underdogs or streaks; Vergin (2001, *Journal of Behavioral Decision Making*) "Overreaction in the NFL point spread market." All of these need checking before citation.
- **Woodland & Woodland:** the only confirmed NBA paper is the season-wins-total study (below). Their well-known favorite-longshot work (*Journal of Finance* 1994) is on MLB/NHL odds (UNVERIFIED for NBA).

## Q2. Documented biases (home underdog, big favorites, hot hand, sentiment, totals, season timing)

### Takeaway
The most replicated NBA anomalies are four. (1) Large favorites are overbet, and big home underdogs (+10 or more) cover too often. (2) Bettors overreact to streaks and momentum. (3) Lines are shaded against popular or home-team sentiment. (4) Totals are biased toward the over and are mispriced early in the season. Sides (spreads) are generally *not* biased early-season.

### Cited Findings
- **Big home underdogs:** In 1995–96 to 2001–02, betting big home dogs rejected the null of no profitability (Paul & Weinbach 2005, *JSE* 6(4)) — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jospec:v:6:y:2005:i:4:p:390-400); [search summary](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/folio/2019/08/how-bookmakers-tip-sports-betting-odds-in-their-favour.html). An out-of-sample retest on 2000–01 to 2007–08 (log-likelihood ratio tests) found "strong support for an efficient market," with one exception: home underdogs of **10+ points** were profitable ("NBA Gambling Inefficiencies: A Second Look," *The Sport Journal*; authors not captured) — [The Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/nba-gambling-inefficiencies-a-second-look/)
- **Favorite bias and sportsbook shading (Levitt-model test):** Paul, R.J. & Weinbach, A.P. (2008), "Price Setting in the NBA Gambling Market: Tests of the Levitt Model of Sportsbook Behavior," *International Journal of Sport Finance* 3(3): 137–145. Using actual betting-percentage data from four sportsbooks (Sports Insights) for 2004–05 to 2006–07, the paper finds that favorites, especially road favorites, get a disproportionate share of bets. Books accept uneven action and build bettor biases into the spread. Unlike in the NFL, simply fading the public does not beat efficiency in the NBA — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/jsf/intjsf/v3y2008i3p137-145.html); [FIT Publishing](https://fitpublishing.com/node/944); [PDF copy](https://kylewoodward.com/blog-data/pdfs/references/paul+weinbach-international-journal-of-sport-finance-2008A.pdf)
- **Popular-team / sentiment bias:** Feddersen, A., Humphreys, B.R. & Soebbing, B.P. (2016), "Sentiment Bias in National Basketball Association Betting," *Journal of Sports Economics*. Using about three decades of NBA spreads and outcomes, the authors find that bookmakers raise prices on bets involving **popular home teams**. Bettors who avoid popular favorites or popular underdogs still do not profit over time — [University of Alberta Folio](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/folio/2019/08/how-bookmakers-tip-sports-betting-odds-in-their-favour.html); [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2019-08-bookmakers-capitalize-fans-emotions-profit.pdf)
- **Point-spread shading:** A Humphreys paper on shading and behavioural biases in the NBA (*Rivista di Diritto ed Economia dello Sport*) reports "clear evidence that sports books shade point spreads," with bettors favouring favorites. Only a snippet was seen — [RDES PDF](https://www.rdes.it/rdes_1_10_humphreys.pdf)
- **Hot hand / momentum:**
  - Waggoner, B., Wines, D., Soebbing, B.P., Seifried, C.S. & Martinez, J.M. (2014), "'Hot Hand' in the National Basketball Association Point Spread Betting Market: A 34-Year Analysis," *International Journal of Financial Studies* 2(4): 359–370. A seemingly unrelated regression (SUR) model over 34 regular seasons finds that streaks affect **both** the point spread and the actual point difference — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jijfss/v2y2014i4p359-370d42745.html); [LSU repository](https://repository.lsu.edu/kinesiology_pubs/326)
  - Arkes, J. (2011), "Finally, Evidence for a Momentum Effect in the NBA," *Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports* 7(3), art. 13. The paper finds real momentum, but gamblers "significantly overstate" its importance — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bpj/jqsprt/v7y2011i3n13.html)
  - A 2011 NBA study in the *Journal of Gambling Business and Economics* 5(2): 42–56 found that betting against the hot hand does not win more than efficiency implies, yet teams on streaks attract significantly more bets — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/buc/jgbeco/v5y2011i2p42-56.html)
  - Research on 1983–86 (the Camerer-era data) found momentum moved the line but not outcomes, though the error was "too small to be profitably exploited" ("The Effect of Momentum on the NBA Point Spread Market," *The Sport Journal*) — [The Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/the-effect-of-momentum-on-the-nba-point-spread-market/)
  - Moskowitz, T.J. (2021), "Asset Pricing and Sports Betting," *Journal of Finance* 76(6): 3153–3209. Across NBA, NFL, MLB and NHL (NBA sample: 18,681 games), he finds strong momentum consistent with *delayed overreaction*, a weaker value effect, and returns too small to overcome transaction costs. He himself says statistical significance is slight — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:76:y:2021:i:6:p:3153-3209); [Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/what-sports-betting-teaches-us-about-financial-markets); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/value-momentum-sports-betting/)
- **Totals (over/under):**
  - Paul, R.J., Weinbach, A.P. & Wilson, M. (2004), "Efficient Markets, Fair Bets, and Profitability in NBA Totals 1995–96 to 2001–02," *Quarterly Review of Economics and Finance*. Evan Moore later commented with newer data (*QREF* 82, 2021, pp. 26–29) — [IDEAS (Moore comment)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/quaeco/v82y2021icp26-29.html)
  - Borghesi, R., Paul, R.J. & Weinbach, A.P. (2010), *Journal of Prediction Markets* 4(2): 15–22. Gamblers strongly prefer the over, and books shade totals upward. The paper covers totals in several sports — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/buc/jpredm/v4y2010i2p15-22.html)
- **Early-season bias:**
  - Baryla, E.A., Borghesi, R.A., Dare, W.H. & Dennis, S.A. (2007), "Learning, Price Formation and the Early Season Bias in the NBA" (ETSU record; believed published in *Finance Research Letters*). NBA **totals** lines are significantly biased early each season, but **sides** (spread) lines are not — [ETSU](https://dc.etsu.edu/etsu-works/17780)
  - "Early-season NBA over/under bias" (University of West Florida record; 2009–2012 data) finds the totals market very efficient overall but inefficient in week 1, when **58.2%** of games finished under the total — [UWF IR Commons](https://ircommons.uwf.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Early-season-NBA-overunder-bias/99380090347306600)
- **Fatigue / travel:** Ashman, T., Bowman, R.A. & Lambrinos, J. (2010), *Journal of Sports Economics* 11(6): 602–613. This paper tests whether the market prices fatigue. Poor performance was magnified when the home team had travelled one or two time zones eastward between back-to-back games — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jospec:v:11:y:2010:i:6:p:602-613)
- **Letdown after clinching:** NBA teams that have locked in a specific seed show evidence of inefficiency from letdown bias (*Financial Services Review* / UGA open journal) — [UGA](https://openjournals.libs.uga.edu/fsr/article/download/3242/2876/9324)
- **Season win totals:** Woodland, B.M. & Woodland, L.M. (2015), "Testing Profitability in the NBA Season Wins Total Betting Market," *International Journal of Sport Finance* 10(2): 160–174. Profitable strategies remain on both overs and unders, though this market is slightly more efficient than the NFL equivalent — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/jsf/intjsf/v10y2015i2p160-174.html)
- **Race composition (working paper):** An MPRA working paper reports that teams with more Black players face larger spreads and do worse ATS, and the authors attribute this to biased bookmaker spreads. Its peer-review status is not confirmed — [MPRA 31469](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/31469/1/MPRA_paper_31469.pdf)

### Inferences
- These biases fit Levitt's model: books shade against predictable public preferences (favorites, road favorites, popular home teams, overs). The shading is usually just big enough to profit from the biased side without leaving the contrarian side above 52.4%.
- The most durable "edges" sit in thin or derivative markets (early-season totals, season win totals, live betting) rather than in mainstream closing sides.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed NBA paper confirmed a **national-TV-game** bias. UNVERIFIED: I recall NFL work on primetime overs (Paul & Weinbach), but nothing confirmed for the NBA.
- Exact ATS win rates (for example, the % for +10 home dogs) were not visible in the snippets.

## Q3. Do anomalies survive the −110 vig and persist out of sample? Evidence of decay

### Takeaway
Mostly no. Nearly every NBA anomaly is either too small to beat the 52.38% breakeven or fades in later samples. The clearest survivor is big home underdogs (+10 or more) into the late 2000s. COVID 2020 was a temporary, sharp exception in moneylines.

### Cited Findings
- A five-season NBA study confirmed weak-form efficiency, rejected semi-strong efficiency, and found betting-simulation win rates below 52.4% ("Is the NBA betting market efficient?") — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/27860819/Is_the_NBA_betting_market_efficient); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24131329_Is_the_NBA_betting_market_efficient)
- Paul & Weinbach's 1995–2002 large-favorite/home-dog result held in part out of sample (2000–08), but only for home dogs of 10+ points; the rest of the market was efficient — [The Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/nba-gambling-inefficiencies-a-second-look/)
- In the NFL comparison, inefficiencies "tend to dissipate over time" (Gray & Gray 1997) — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v52y1997i4p1725-37.html)
- Momentum and value returns across four leagues "fail to overcome transactions costs" (Moskowitz 2021) — [Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/what-sports-betting-teaches-us-about-financial-markets)
- Momentum errors in 1983–86 were too small to exploit — [The Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/the-effect-of-momentum-on-the-nba-point-spread-market/)
- Fading the public in the NBA does not beat efficiency (Paul & Weinbach 2008) — [FIT Publishing](https://fitpublishing.com/node/944)
- Avoiding popular teams does not yield long-run profits despite shading (Feddersen, Humphreys & Soebbing 2016) — [UAlberta](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/folio/2019/08/how-bookmakers-tip-sports-betting-odds-in-their-favour.html)
- **COVID-19:** Qureshi & Zaman (arXiv 2109.07581, preprint) find NBA *moneyline* markets became "very inefficient" during the pandemic. Betting every underdog from the start of the bubble to the end of the 2020 season returned **16.7%**, while other major sports stayed efficient — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.07581). By contrast, a European basketball study (*Annals of Operations Research* 2022, De Angelis & Reade) found that books anticipated the roughly 5% drop in home advantage and priced it correctly — [Unibo PDF](https://cris.unibo.it/bitstream/11585/894931/4/s10479-022-04950-7.pdf)
- Contrary evidence: a Miami University thesis (Lust) using ten online sportsbooks and median regression reports profitable strategies, amplified where markups are lower. This is a thesis, not peer-reviewed — [OhioLINK ETD](https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file/send?accession=miami1533158563565605&disposition=inline)

### Inferences
- The size of the vig is pivotal. Reduced-juice or line-shopping settings (as in Lust's thesis) can turn statistically real but tiny biases profitable, while −110 single-book betting typically cannot.
- Anomalies decay roughly as they are published: Camerer's hot-hand effect was untradeable by the late 1980s, and the Paul & Weinbach home-dog effect had narrowed to +10 dogs by the 2000s.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study testing NBA ATS anomalies on **post-PASPA (2018+) US legal-market data**. This remains a key gap.
- Whether the COVID-bubble moneyline inefficiency carried over to spreads is not established.

## Q4. Statistical methods and the meaning of "weak-form efficiency"

### Takeaway
There are three standard test families: (1) the OLS regression Margin = α + β·Spread + ε, with the joint test α=0, β=1; (2) probit/logit models of win-against-spread; and (3) betting simulations judged against the 52.38% breakeven, often with likelihood-ratio tests of "fair bet" (50%) versus "no profitability" (52.38%). Weak form means current spreads cannot be beaten using past price and outcome information alone.

### Cited Findings
- Gandar et al. (1988) distinguish statistical tests, which could not reject rationality, from economic (betting-rule) tests, which did reject it — [scite](https://scite.ai/authors/john-m-gandar-RVlwWp)
- Gray & Gray (1997) replace OLS with probit, which yields significant in-sample profits — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v52y1997i4p1725-37.html)
- Paul & Weinbach (2005) test both the "fair bet" null and the "no profitability" null. Big home dogs reject both — [UAlberta summary / RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jospec:v:6:y:2005:i:4:p:390-400)
- The 2000–08 retest uses log-likelihood ratio tests — [The Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/nba-gambling-inefficiencies-a-second-look/)
- Waggoner et al. (2014) use a seemingly unrelated regression (SUR) of spread and actual margin on streak variables — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jijfss/v2y2014i4p359-370d42745.html)
- Weak-form efficiency is confirmed and semi-strong efficiency rejected in a five-season NBA study, with a 52.4% simulation benchmark — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/27860819/Is_the_NBA_betting_market_efficient)
- Durham & Santhanakrishnan (2012), *JSE* 13(5): 554–566, examine whether changes in the point spread are an acceptable proxy for realized return — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jospec:v:13:y:2012:i:5:p:554-566)

### Inferences
- The 52.38% breakeven follows from −110 pricing (110/210). "Fair bet" tests ask whether the spread is unbiased (50%). "Profitability" tests ask whether a rule beats 52.38%. A market can fail the first test and still be economically efficient.

### Gaps
- **SD of the margin around the closing spread:** only an informal blog confirms a figure of about 12 points ([Goodreads blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/16483561-why-is-standard-deviation-of-nba-scores-about-point-spread-12-points)). UNVERIFIED: commonly cited academic estimates are about 11–12 points (Stern-style normal approximations), but I found no peer-reviewed source for it here.
- **R² of spreads as predictors of NBA margins:** no source found. UNVERIFIED recollection is roughly 0.2–0.25 at the single-game level; this needs a primary source.

## Q5. Levitt (2004) and the balanced-book question in the NBA

### Takeaway
Levitt shows that bookmakers do not balance books. They take positions and shade lines against predictable bettor biases. NBA betting-percentage studies confirm the same behaviour for basketball.

### Cited Findings
- Levitt, S.D. (2004), "Why Are Gambling Markets Organised So Differently from Financial Markets?", *Economic Journal* 114(495): 223–246. Bookmakers predict outcomes better than bettors and set prices away from the market-clearing level to exploit bettor biases. They take large positions rather than acting as matchmakers. The data are NFL wagers from 285 bettors in an online handicapping contest — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ecj/econjl/v114y2004i495p223-246.html); [PDF](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/users/aldous/157/Papers/Levitt_Gambling_2004.pdf); [NBER w9422](https://www.nber.org/papers/w9422)
- NBA test: Paul & Weinbach (2008, *IJSF*) use 2004–05 to 2006–07 Sports Insights data and confirm unbalanced action (heavy on favorites and road favorites) priced into spreads — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/jsf/intjsf/v3y2008i3p137-145.html)
- A University of Alberta working paper (2010) cites NBA 2004–06 volume data as challenging the balanced-book model — [UAlberta WP 2010-05](https://sites.ualberta.ca/~econwps/2010/wp2010-05.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a modern NBA bettor, sharp lines are shaded *toward* public sides, so the contrarian side is fairly priced but not +EV after vig.

### Gaps
- Levitt's own data are NFL only. The NBA extension rests on Paul & Weinbach and later work.

## Q6. Recent (2015–2026) studies, large datasets, post-PASPA

### Takeaway
Recent scholarship (Moskowitz 2021 JF; Feddersen et al. 2016 JSE; Waggoner et al. 2014) uses multi-decade datasets and finds behavioural patterns (momentum overreaction, sentiment shading) that are statistically real but not exploitable after costs. Peer-reviewed analysis of post-PASPA US legal-book NBA spreads is largely absent from what I could find.

### Cited Findings
- Moskowitz (2021, *JF*): 18,681 NBA games, about 120,000 contracts across leagues; momentum (delayed overreaction) is not exploitable — [BFI](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/?p=4342); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/value-momentum-sports-betting/)
- Feddersen, Humphreys & Soebbing (2016, *JSE*): three decades of NBA data; sentiment shading toward popular home teams — [UAlberta](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/folio/2019/08/how-bookmakers-tip-sports-betting-odds-in-their-favour.html)
- Qureshi & Zaman (2021 preprint): NBA COVID-bubble underdog moneyline +16.7% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.07581)
- Moore (2021), *QREF* 82: 26–29, revisits NBA totals efficiency with newer data — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/quaeco/v82y2021icp26-29.html)
- Hubáček, Šourek & Železný (or co-authors), "Optimal sports betting strategies in practice: an experimental review" (arXiv 2107.08827): a CNN trained on about 16,000 NBA games (2000–2015) against **Pinnacle closing lines** produced a negative return (about −1.5%) on moneylines — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08827) (author list not confirmed from snippet)
- A 2026 arXiv paper on Polymarket NBA prediction markets finds single-market anomalies "exceedingly rare," consistent with high efficiency — [arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00864v1)
- An arXiv paper, "Beating the House: Identifying Inefficiencies in Sports Betting Markets" (1910.08858), includes NBA results; its findings were not readable from the snippet — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.08858)

### Inferences
- The broad consensus across four decades is that NBA closing spreads are weak-form efficient net of vig. Documented edges are niche (big home dogs, early-season totals, thin derivative markets), episodic (COVID 2020), or exist only at low-margin books or with line shopping.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed post-PASPA (2018–2026) NBA ATS efficiency study was found. Also not found: studies of the 3-point-era scoring surge (higher totals variance) and its effect on spread SD.
- I could not verify how strong the Pinnacle closing line is as an NBA benchmark beyond the arXiv experimental review.
